Pitch Dynamics Model

Overview

The model describes a simplified aircraft's pitch dynamics using two states:

  • Pitch angle θ [rad]
  • Pitch rate q [rad/s]

The elevator deflection δe acts as the control input.

The model is:

dθ/dt = q

dq/dt = K·δe - D·q

where:

  • K — elevator effectiveness
  • D — pitch damping
  • δe — elevator deflection

Physics

The elevator does not directly change the pitch angle or pitch rate. Its aerodynamic effect produces a pitching moment about the aircraft's centre of gravity.

The rotational equivalent of Newton's second law is:

Iy·dq/dt = My

The simplified model absorbs the aircraft's inertia and aerodynamic properties into the coefficients K and D.

The resulting chain is:

elevator deflection
        ↓
  pitching moment
        ↓
 pitch acceleration
        ↓
    pitch rate
        ↓
   pitch angle

Elevator effectiveness

The term:

K·δe

represents the pitching acceleration generated by the elevator.

A larger elevator deflection produces a larger pitching moment and therefore a larger change in pitch rate.

Pitch damping

The term:

-D·q

represents aerodynamic resistance to rotation.

The faster the aircraft pitches, the stronger the damping moment opposing that rotation. Therefore, damping always acts in the opposite direction to q.

For a constant elevator deflection, the pitch rate eventually reaches an equilibrium where:

K·δe - D·q = 0

Therefore:

q = K·δe / D

Thus, a constant elevator deflection produces a constant pitch rate, rather than a constant pitch angle.

Numerical State Propagation

The continuous dynamics must be integrated numerically to advance the model through discrete simulation timesteps.

First calculate the pitch acceleration:

dq/dt = K·δe - D·q_prev

Then update the pitch rate:

q_new = q_prev + (dq/dt)·dt

Finally update the pitch angle:

θ_new = θ_prev + q_new·dt

Excursion into Numerical Integration Methods

The final equation uses the newly calculated pitch rate. This is so-called semi-implicit Euler integration.

Using the previous pitch rate instead:

θ_new = θ_prev + q_prev·dt

would be explicit Euler integration.

For this simple model, both methods work with a sufficiently small timestep, but semi-implicit Euler generally provides better numerical behaviour for mechanical systems. Explicit Euler can add artificial energy to systems, leading to instability. To mitigate this, semi-implicit Euler can be used, as it tends to be stable and energy preserving.
